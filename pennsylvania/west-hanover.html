Local housing age matters, too: with about 40.1% of homes built before 1980 (U.S. Census Bureau, ACS 2024), insulation gaps and older duct runs are common. When a home needs more tonnage or airflow, you’re not just buying higher-SEER2 or better AFUE—you’re often paying for additional labor, testing, and balancing to make the equipment work efficiently on day one.

Permits & regulations in Pennsylvania

In Pennsylvania, HVAC work is regulated to protect homeowners and ensure safe handling of gas and refrigerants. First, HVAC contractors must hold a Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) registration through the Pennsylvania Attorney General’s Office. If the job includes gas furnace or gas piping work, the installing technician must hold an active PA gas piping license. For AC and heat pump refrigerant handling, technicians must be EPA Section 608 certified—ask to see the technician’s EPA 608 card before work begins.

Mechanical permits are required by most PA municipalities for HVAC replacement and new installation. A permit is typically not required for like-for-like filter changes or thermostat swaps, but the moment you replace equipment, modify ducting, or run new refrigerant piping, you should expect permit and inspection steps. For rebates, check the PA Homeowner Rebate program and utility rebates (including PPL Electric, PECO, and Columbia Gas where applicable) for qualifying high-efficiency heat pumps and furnaces under IRA Inflation Reduction Act program rules.

For verification, start with: (1) PA HIC registration at hic.attorneygeneral.gov, (2) the EPA 608 certificate from the technician, and (3) certificate of insurance plus proof of workers’ compensation. In West Hanover, a responsible contractor will provide these documents before scheduling. Step-by-step: request a COI and WC proof, confirm the HIC name matches the proposal contract, then confirm the technician listed on the job has the EPA 608 card tied to the refrigerant work being performed.

Heat pump vs. furnace + AC — what makes sense in West Hanover?

Pennsylvania’s four-season climate makes the heat pump decision more nuanced than in milder states. Winters in West Hanover regularly drop below freezing, while summers are hot and humid—so you need both heating capacity and solid dehumidification performance. Modern cold-climate air source heat pumps (CC-ASHP) can operate efficiently down to about -13°F, making them viable across most of Dauphin County. However, in older West Hanover homes—where about 40.1% were built before 1980 (U.S. Census Bureau, ACS 2024)—duct leakage and insulation gaps can reduce delivered comfort and efficiency long before the first utility bill arrives.

That’s why the “right” system is often the one that matches your distribution setup. If you have an aging central AC and reliable ducts, pairing replacement equipment can make sense; if you have rooms that are hard to balance or you’re battling duct issues, a ductless or properly designed heat pump setup can outperform a conventional dual system. In practice, a West Hanover homeowner might compare a dual system (gas furnace + central AC) versus a ducted cold-climate heat pump. If the heat pump quote is in the $7,000–$12,000 range and the dual system is around $7,000–$12,000 combined, the justification for heat pump usually comes from reducing fuel reliance and taking advantage of incentives—especially when insulation and duct sealing improve overall airflow.

If your home is drafty or ducted distribution is weak, duct repairs or zoning may be needed first; otherwise, even the best-rated equipment can’t overcome comfort complaints. The best next step is a load calculation and duct evaluation so you’re not paying to “buy efficiency” that the home can’t deliver.

How to choose a HVAC installation contractor in West Hanover

Choosing the right contractor matters as much as choosing the equipment. Start by verifying licensing and credentials. For HVAC contractors, confirm Pennsylvania HIC registration through hic.attorneygeneral.gov. If the job includes gas appliances, confirm the relevant technician holds the active PA gas piping license. For any AC/heat pump refrigerant work, request the technician’s EPA Section 608 certification card in advance—reputable crews don’t hesitate to show it.

Next, get 2–3 itemized written quotes rather than one lump-sum number. A good quote breaks out labor and materials, lists the indoor and outdoor model numbers, states whether duct sealing or duct replacement is included, and documents what’s excluded (for example, electrical panel upgrades, venting changes, or condenser pad work). Scope clarity prevents disputes later.

Warranties should be written and specific: ask for the workmanship warranty length and whether equipment warranties are provided by the manufacturer with the correct registration steps. Payment scheduling should be controlled—never pay more than about 10–15% upfront, and plan to hold back payment until the job is complete, tested, and cleaned up.

Finally, timeline communication should be in writing. Ask for an arrival estimate and a completion window, because peak-season lead times in the region commonly run 1–3 weeks for full installs.

What is a SEER2 rating and why does it matter in West Hanover?

SEER2 is an efficiency rating for central air conditioners and ducted cooling systems. In West Hanover, it matters because you’re running AC during humid stretches and you want equipment that keeps electrical use reasonable while maintaining indoor comfort. A higher SEER2 (for example, many modern central replacements fall around the 16–20 SEER2 range) generally means better performance—especially when paired with correct airflow and duct sealing. If your home has older ductwork (common in pre-1980 housing stock), low airflow can reduce real-world cooling efficiency, even if the condenser is high-SEER2. That’s why the best installations in Dauphin County pair equipment selection with duct evaluation, not just a “swap the outdoor unit” approach.

Budget-wise, central AC replacements in the area are often estimated around $4,800–$12,500, but the total depends on duct condition and electrical scope. A contractor should explain whether you’re simply replacing the condenser or also addressing airflow and duct leakage that can otherwise erase some efficiency gains.

How much does a new HVAC system cost in West Hanover, PA?

New HVAC cost in West Hanover typically depends on whether you’re replacing an existing system with similar ductwork, adding ductwork, or moving to ductless/heat pump solutions. For example, central AC replacement (with existing ducts) commonly lands in the $4,800–$12,500 band, while ducted air source heat pump installations often price in the $7,000–$12,000 range based on equipment size, efficiency tier, and install complexity. If you need a full duct system where ducts don’t exist or you’re doing a gut-rehab distribution change, full duct install projects can fall around $3,200–$11,000, depending on square footage and layout.

In Dauphin County’s older housing stock, duct repairs can be a hidden driver—sometimes you start expecting a straightforward AC swap and then uncover undersized returns, leaky trunk lines, or airflow imbalances. Also remember Pennsylvania’s permitting and inspection steps for equipment replacement can add modest cost. The best way to get a firm number is an on-site inspection with an itemized proposal.

Is a heat pump a good choice for West Hanover's winters?

Often, yes—especially with a modern cold-climate air source heat pump and proper system design. West Hanover and the broader Dauphin County area experience winter temperatures that require equipment to perform below freezing. Cold-climate heat pumps are engineered for those conditions and can operate efficiently down to around -13°F, which makes them viable across much of the region when installed correctly.

That said, older homes can affect performance. About 40.1% of homes locally were built before 1980 (U.S. Census Bureau, ACS 2024), and older insulation/duct leakage can reduce effective heat delivery and dehumidification control. If ducts are leaky or room distribution is poor, you may need duct sealing, airflow balancing, or a dual-fuel strategy (heat pump plus gas backup below a threshold) to prevent uncomfortable indoor temperatures.

Cost comparisons help: a ducted cold-climate heat pump may be in the $7,000–$12,000 range, while a dual system with gas furnace + central AC can be similar or higher depending on your existing setup. The “good choice” comes down to insulation, duct condition, and whether incentives apply.

How long does HVAC installation take in West Hanover?

Most HVAC installations in West Hanover are completed in a single workweek day plan, but the real schedule depends on system type and permitting/inspection timing. For a typical replacement with existing ductwork and straightforward electrical service, the crew may install and commission the equipment in about one to three days, with additional time for duct sealing, refrigerant line routing, and electrical checks. If you’re adding ductwork, moving to ductless mini-splits, or doing a bigger heat pump conversion, the timeline can expand because of wall/floor access, line-set routing, and more extensive testing.

Peak season in the region can add lead time—often about 1–3 weeks for complete installs—because equipment availability and scheduling are busy. Permits and inspections can also affect the date the system is fully signed off. If a job involves mechanical permits, inspections may be required before final approval.

Ask your contractor for an arrival estimate and a written completion window. A responsible company also explains any potential “hold points,” like waiting on an electrical inspection after panel or breaker changes.

What size HVAC system do I need for my West Hanover home?

The correct HVAC size for your West Hanover home should be based on a proper load calculation—not just square footage. In older Dauphin County housing, two homes of the same size can need different system capacity because insulation levels, duct leakage, window types, and air infiltration vary widely—especially in pre-1980 dwellings where about 40.1% of homes were built (U.S. Census Bureau, ACS 2024). If a system is oversized, it can short-cycle, feel cool but not dehumidify well, and wear out sooner. If it’s undersized, it may struggle during winter cold snaps and humid summer peaks.

A good contractor should evaluate your ductwork and perform (or at least document) a sizing approach that accounts for heating and cooling loads, plus airflow targets. If you’re comparing options like central AC versus ducted heat pump, duct leakage can change the delivered airflow and effective capacity, which can shift the correct equipment size. That’s one reason quotes—even within price bands such as $7,000–$12,000 for ducted heat pumps—can’t be truly finalized without a site assessment.

When you get quotes, ask what measurement they used, what assumptions they made, and how duct condition and insulation influenced the final tonnage/BTU selection.
