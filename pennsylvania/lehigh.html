How long does an HVAC system last in Pennsylvania?

What size AC system do I need for my Lehigh home?
Proper AC sizing is critical — oversized systems short-cycle and cause humidity problems; undersized systems can't keep up on hot PA days. The correct method is a Manual J load calculation performed by your licensed Lehigh HVAC contractor, which accounts for your home's square footage, insulation, window area, ceiling height, and local climate data. As a rough guide: 1 ton per 600-1,000 sq ft, but don't let a contractor quote based on square footage alone without a proper load calculation.

Permits & regulations in Pennsylvania

In Pennsylvania, HVAC installation and replacement are regulated to protect homeowners and ensure safe work. In most cases, Pennsylvania requires HVAC contractors to hold a Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) registration through the Pennsylvania Attorney General’s Office—verify this at hic.attorneygeneral.gov. For gas appliance installs (like gas furnace replacement), the installing technician must hold an active PA gas piping license. For any AC or heat pump work that involves refrigerant, the technician must also be EPA Section 608 certified—ask to see the technician’s EPA 608 card before the job starts.

Mechanical permits are required by many PA municipalities for HVAC replacement and new installation. A permit is typically not required for like-for-like filter changes or thermostat swaps, but replacing the unit, changing significant components, or installing new ductless/ducted equipment commonly triggers permit requirements. Always verify with your local township or borough office (or the municipal building department) for the exact rules where you live in the Lehigh Valley.

For incentives, check the PA Homeowner Rebate program and utility rebates (including PPL Electric, PECO, and Columbia Gas) for qualifying high-efficiency heat pumps and furnaces under IRA-related pathways. ENERGY STAR equipment may also qualify for federal tax credits up to 30% under Section 25C.

Step-by-step: (1) confirm the contractor’s HIC registration online, (2) request the EPA 608 card for refrigerant-handling techs, (3) review the contractor’s certificate of insurance, and (4) ask for proof of workers’ compensation coverage. If you can’t get clear documentation on these items, it’s a reason to pause—especially in older Lehigh Valley homes where electrical coordination, duct modifications, and condensate routing can add scope.

What is a SEER2 rating and why does it matter in Lehigh?

SEER2 (Seasonal Energy Efficiency Ratio 2) is a measure of how efficiently an air conditioning system cools over a typical season. In plain terms, a higher SEER2 means the unit generally uses less electricity to move the same amount of cooling capacity—assuming it’s properly sized and installed with correct airflow. In Lehigh and Northampton County, where summers are humid and cooling demand can be intense, SEER2 helps translate into lower operating costs, especially when equipment runs frequently.

Why installation matters: if ductwork leaks or the system doesn’t have the right airflow (wrong static pressure, poor return balance, or undersized ducts), efficiency won’t deliver as advertised. That’s common in pre-1980 homes across the Lehigh Valley, where 52.8% of housing stock is older than 1980. Even a high-SEER2 system can feel disappointing if the house isn’t getting the conditioned air it needs.

When choosing among options, pay attention to SEER2 tiering. Central AC replacement typically lands in the 16–20 SEER2 range, and ductless systems often fall in the 20–30 SEER2 range depending on the model. If you’re considering $4,500–$12,500 central AC replacement versus a higher tier option, ask your contractor to explain sizing and airflow—because the best SEER2 value comes from matching the equipment to your actual duct/airflow realities.
