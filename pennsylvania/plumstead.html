Permits & regulations in Pennsylvania

In Pennsylvania, HVAC installation isn’t just a comfort decision—it’s a compliance checklist. Contractors must be properly registered and licensed. Pennsylvania requires HVAC contractors to hold a Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) registration through the Pennsylvania Attorney General’s Office. For gas appliances, the technician installing the gas piping must hold an active PA gas piping license. For AC and heat pump refrigeration work, technicians must have EPA Section 608 certification—so ask to see the technician’s EPA 608 card before work starts.

Most PA municipalities also require mechanical permits for HVAC replacement and new installation. A permit is typically not required for like-for-like items such as thermostat swaps or routine filter changes, but once you’re changing equipment location, venting/flue routing, electrical feeds, or ductwork substantially, permits become very likely. Verify the permit requirements with your local township or borough office (Plumstead homeowners should confirm locally).

Rebates and tax credits can reduce effective cost. Check Pennsylvania Homeowner Rebate programs and utility rebates (PPL Electric, PECO, Columbia Gas) for qualifying high-efficiency heat pumps and furnaces under IRA-related programs. ENERGY STAR certified equipment may also qualify for federal tax credits up to 30% under Section 25C.

To verify credentials step-by-step: (1) confirm the company’s PA HIC registration at hic.attorneygeneral.gov; (2) request the technician’s EPA 608 card; (3) obtain and review a certificate of insurance (liability) and workers’ compensation (WC) proof; (4) ask the contractor whether they will pull the mechanical permit and schedule the inspection, and confirm inspection details in writing.

Next, require 2–3 itemized written quotes. For example, does the quote include electrical disconnect changes, condensate drain line corrections, any duct sealing or return-air additions, and start-up/commissioning?

Warranty and payment timing matter. Ask for: (1) the workmanship warranty length; (2) the equipment/manufacturer warranty details; and (3) whether the contractor will provide a start-up report and performance verification. For payment scheduling, don’t pay more than 10–15% upfront; hold back retainage until the job is complete and you’ve confirmed operation. Timeline-wise, insist on an arrival estimate and a completion window in writing.
