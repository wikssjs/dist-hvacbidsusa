What a new HVAC system costs in Murrysville

Murrysville homeowners looking at new HVAC installation land in a unique bracket. With nearly 90% homeownership and most homes built before 1980 (U.S. Census Bureau, ACS 2024), this is not a market of blank-slate new builds—it's one full of existing homes that have already gone through an initial round of ductwork upgrades and electrical conversions, then a second (or even third) round of equipment swaps. Most HVAC projects here are either full replacements or retrofits, not first-time installs.

These homes generally have existing ductwork, though the duct's condition and sizing can vary widely, depending on how and when earlier upgrades happened. Favorable gas availability means many homes started with either oil or gas heat, later adding central air or, more recently, heat pumps. With our climate pushing 5,600 annual heating degree days and only 1,050 cooling degree days, Murrysville is solidly a heating-dominated market. That tips the balance: your winter comfort needs and heating costs will structure the conversation even if your main concern is “cooler summers.”

Because most homes already have ducts, a central AC or ducted heat pump makes sense for the majority. However, if the ducts are unsalvageable or missing (not rare in additions or pre-1960s homes), a mini-split or an all-new duct system becomes part of the conversation. Filtration, humidification and ventilation

Murrysville’s long heating season means the main indoor air complaint is dryness from October to April, followed by sticky summers. The solution set is different for each problem — and much of the gear marketed for air “purity” isn’t doing what’s promised.

In winter, a whole-house humidifier can be a comfort upgrade, but only if it’s matched to the size of your HVAC blower and properly maintained. They require regular cleaning and annual pad or canister changes. Skip the upgrade if you’re not willing to keep up with this, or it will become a source of mold and mineral buildup. Don’t be sold an oversized unit "just in case"—that can actually damage your system.

During muggy Murrysville summers, an oversized air conditioner won’t run long enough to pull moisture from the air. That leads to a clammy feeling and potential for mold growth in poorly ventilated areas. The right-sized system, running longer cycles at lower speed, will always out-perform something too big.

When it comes to filtration, a MERV 8 to MERV 11 filter is ideal for most older homes — any higher and you risk reducing airflow if the blower motor isn’t up to the job. Expensive electronic air cleaners and UV lights are usually oversold here, with little demonstrated impact for the average homeowner.

If you’ve tightened up the home envelope or have a newer, well-sealed house, mechanical ventilation (either an energy recovery ventilator or a simple fresh-air intake) is well worth discussing. But for most Murrysville homes, focusing on humidity control and right-sized filtration is where your money does the most good.

What you can actually claim on a heat pump here

For Murrysville homeowners considering a heat pump, three incentive layers are possible: federal tax credits, utility rebates from West Penn Power or Peoples Natural Gas, and any state-level energy efficiency programs. Here’s how these play out.

A tax credit, such as those periodically available through federal policy, lets you reduce what you owe on next year’s taxes — you pay for the system now, claim the credit when you file, and see the benefit months later. In contrast, a rebate from your utility (West Penn Power for electricity, Peoples Natural Gas for gas) will pay out quicker, often as a check or direct discount after installation and documentation.

It’s tempting to chase after specific rebate or incentive numbers, but these change every year. In Pennsylvania, the only way to know what really applies to your replacement or new system is checking direct: head to your local utility’s energy efficiency program page and plug your zip code and equipment details into the ENERGY STAR rebate finder. Details — amounts, requirements, and qualifying products — change every program year, so treat any published figure (even from an “official” article) as a ballpark, not a promise.

The takeaway: don’t buy a system just for an incentive. The base price for a ducted heat pump install in Murrysville runs $8,400–$18,000, and even a generous rebate is one piece of the budget.

Repair or replace your Murrysville system?

This is the hardest question for most Murrysville homeowners, especially when an emergency strikes mid-winter or at the first summer heat wave. The first step is to weigh the system’s age and the scale of the failure against replacement cost. If your equipment is under 10 years old and the problem is a single part — a capacitor, igniter, or fan motor — repair is almost always the answer. Typical costs are much less than a replacement.

For systems between 10 and 15 years old, compare the price of the repair to about a third of a new replacement. If repair costs are creeping above that mark, a new system will usually make more sense. Once past the 15-year mark, any major failure is a sign it’s time to plan for replacement — let alone at the 20-year mark, which is the real-world ceiling for most units in our freeze-thaw climate.

Two situations mean you cannot just fix and move on: a cracked heat exchanger (in a gas furnace), and a failed compressor on an older AC or heat pump. Both signal the end of safe, reliable service for that unit. Likewise, if your existing system uses a refrigerant that’s no longer produced, repair costs can spiral quickly, making replacement more cost-effective.

Maintenance that actually extends system life

Plenty gets called “maintenance,” but only a handful of tasks truly matter for system life in Murrysville’s mix of older homes and cold-weather runtime.

First, change your filters on time and use the right MERV grade for your system. Most single-family homes here are safest with MERV 8–11; higher numbers may choke airflow if the blower isn’t up for it. Regular filter swaps—typically every three months, monthly in heavy use—are foundational.

Next, make sure your indoor and outdoor coils are kept clean, especially after pollen season and fall leaf drop. Clogged coils reduce efficiency and system life. The condensate drain line needs to be cleared every spring, or you risk water leaks and basement mildew.

On refrigerant: if your system needs a top-off, there’s a leak. Proper maintenance means finding and fixing that leak, not just adding more each year. “Top-ups” without a leak fix are a recurring expense and an environmental issue.

Annual service plans are sometimes worthwhile, but only if they include actual checks—refrigerant charge (using gauges), airflow, safety controls, and a written report. If your plan is just a rushed filter change and a sales pitch, it’s not worth the fee. In Murrysville, schedule maintenance in early spring or early fall to avoid heat-wave and mid-winter emergencies.

Choosing an installer in Westmoreland County

How your HVAC system is installed matters more than which brand goes in—a fact that’s easy to overlook in the sea of ads Murrysville homeowners receive each year. In this moderate suburban Pittsburgh market, every truck claims “factory-trained,” but performance, efficiency, and longevity are mostly about details at install—not the badge on the unit itself.

Begin with the basics: check their Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) registration at the state Attorney General’s site, and verify their workers' compensation coverage through WCAIS. Anyone handling refrigerants (even for a simple AC swap) must provide proof of EPA Section 608 certification. You want to see a full load calculation (not just square footage and a guess), clear model and serial numbers on each piece of quoted equipment, and commissioning documentation—measured airflow, static pressures, and refrigerant charge—when the job’s finished.

  • Look up HIC registration at hic.attorneygeneral.gov
  • Check workers' comp coverage through WCAIS (wcais.pa.gov)
  • Ask for EPA Section 608 certification for anyone handling refrigerants
  • Insist on a written, signed contract (required by law over $500)
  • Require a manual J (load calculation) specific to your home
  • Review actual model numbers and efficiency ratings in your quote
  • Confirm mechanical permits will be pulled with your township
  • Request commissioning data (airflow and refrigerant charge)
  • Get confirmation of start-to-finish project supervision
  • Check references for work on older homes (built before 1980)
  • Verify warranty details in writing
  • Never pay in full before work begins

Red flags locally? In a “moderate” market like Westmoreland County, unreliable contractors don’t last long, but you still see unregistered pop-ups chasing quick work. If they can’t supply their HIC number, proof of insurance, or a township permit, keep looking—there are enough serious companies here to give you a complete bid.

Mechanical permits for HVAC work in Murrysville

In Pennsylvania, there is no state-issued HVAC license for residential work. Instead, HVAC contractors in Murrysville and across Westmoreland County must carry Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) registration, verified by the state Attorney General for any contract over $500. Local municipalities—including Murrysville—handle mechanical permitting and inspections, not the state. Always verify directly with your township office what requires a permit: generally, any system replacement, new install, or significant duct alteration needs one.

Anyone handling refrigerant—as in all AC and heat pump installations—must also hold federal EPA Section 608 certification. This is non-negotiable, regardless of state or local licensing. It ensures technicians are trained and certified to handle refrigerant legally and safely.

When hiring, ask to see proof of general liability insurance and workers’ compensation coverage. Pennsylvania uses the WCAIS system for workers’ comp verification—homeowners can check coverage through wcais.pa.gov. For liability insurance, request a certificate directly from the contractor’s agent with your name as the certificate holder.

Don’t proceed without a written contract: the law requires it on anything over $500, and it protects both you and the contractor. Permit sign-off from the township is the final step to confirm the work was inspected and meets the Pennsylvania Uniform Construction Code.

Frequently asked questions — HVAC installation in Murrysville

Can a heat pump heat my house without backup? For most well-insulated Murrysville homes, a modern cold-climate heat pump can handle our winter lows—which average 8 °F design days on code charts—without constant help from backup heat. Ducted cold-climate units on the higher end of efficiency (9–11 HSPF2) are designed for our 5,600 annual heating degree days. Still, truly backup-free setups usually need a careful load calculation, good ductwork, and newer windows and insulation. Many postwar homes locally are borderline: either the heat pump covers 90%+ of the season, or it needs resistance heat (built into most air handlers) on the coldest mornings. Be wary of budget heat pumps that can’t sustain our coldest snap—expect to invest toward the upper end of the installed price band: $13,500–$18,000 for a robust, all-electric system with no fossil backup.
What size HVAC system does my Murrysville home need? System size depends on your home’s age, insulation, orientation, and square footage—not just the number of bedrooms. Murrysville's housing stock is largely pre-1980, meaning insulation and window upgrades can vary. Oversizing is common in these homes (especially after window and insulation retrofits), leading to short cycling and moisture problems. A reputable contractor will use an actual Manual J load calculation for your specific address, not a “rule of thumb.” As a ballpark, a typical four-bedroom, 2,200-square-foot home here lands somewhere between 2.5 and 4 tons—but that’s a starting point. Picking the wrong size can erase any investment in efficiency, even on systems in the $8,400–$18,000 install range.
How much does a new HVAC system cost in Murrysville? Pricing in Murrysville is a function of retrofit complexity more than brand: expect a range of $5,300–$12,500 for a standard replacement of a central AC using existing ductwork. Need a full duct system or a major electrical upgrade? That jumps to $9,500–$19,000. Mini-split (ductless) installs for additions or rooms with no ducts fall between $3,400–$7,400 per zone. The biggest variables aren’t the sticker price on the equipment, but ductwork condition, electrical panel size, and what local codes demand for permits and inspections.
Is natural gas available throughout Westmoreland County? Natural gas is widely available for heating and appliances in most developed parts of Westmoreland County, including Murrysville and other major townships. Peoples Natural Gas serves the area, making it an option for most replacements and conversions. However, if you're in a more rural pocket or a home on the county border, you may find propane or oil systems in place of natural gas. Be sure to check with Peoples Natural Gas or talk to neighbors before assuming a gas conversion is possible. Where gas isn’t practical, the main options run in the $8,400–$18,000 installed range for heat pumps or electric replacements.
How do I verify an HVAC contractor's insurance? In Pennsylvania, and specifically in Murrysville, all HVAC contractors are required to carry general liability insurance and workers’ compensation, with the latter verified through the WCAIS system (wcais.pa.gov). When getting a quote, ask for a certificate of insurance—your name and address should be listed as the certificate holder. Don’t accept an expired document or generic proof. For workers’ comp, the WCAIS site allows anyone to confirm active coverage using a contractor’s name or number. This is vital protection as you’re liable if an uninsured worker is injured on your property. Many legitimate companies offering replacements in the $5,300–$12,500 range will provide this upfront; if one hesitates, move on.
Why does my system short cycle? Short cycling—where your HVAC turns on and off in quick bursts—is a common issue in older Murrysville homes, especially after window or insulation upgrades. Often, it means the system is oversized for current needs. This was typical for homes built before 1980, where contractors oversized equipment to “guarantee” comfort. Other causes could be dirty filters, an improperly set thermostat, or air flow issues from duct problems. Short cycling not only wastes energy, it speeds up wear. A full load calculation and duct test—as part of a system replacement or repair in the $300–$1,200 range—can pinpoint the fix, whether it’s downsizing at replacement or tweaking your current setup.
Do I need ENERGY STAR equipment to qualify for incentives? Many incentives for heat pump or high-efficiency HVAC upgrades in Murrysville require ENERGY STAR or a certain minimum efficiency rating (like SEER2 or HSPF2), but not all rebates and tax credits are limited only to ENERGY STAR-branded units. The critical piece is matching your equipment’s specs and the installation date with the requirements posted on your specific utility’s or ENERGY STAR’s rebate program at the time you purchase. Rules do change every program year, and some local rebates in the $8,400–$18,000 price range only apply to equipment that lists specific efficiency levels, not just the logo. Always confirm before you sign, as qualifying models and paperwork are the difference between cash back and missing out.
Why do HVAC quotes in Murrysville vary so much? In Murrysville, HVAC quotes range widely—sometimes by several thousand dollars—because older homes bring uneven ductwork, hidden electrical needs, and corners cut or added in past retrofits. One quote for a system replacement might seem low but doesn’t include new duct runs or electrical work. Another may price for every possible code upgrade. Market density here means there are everything from one-truck contractors to larger firms, each with different overhead and approaches. Equipment brand is much less of a cost factor than duct system condition and code compliance. Expect replacement quotes to land between $5,300–$12,500 for standard swaps, but ask for line item details to compare apples to apples.
When is the best time of year to replace HVAC in Murrysville? The best timing in Murrysville for an HVAC replacement is early spring (March–April) or early fall (September–October). These window periods let you avoid the rush of emergency calls during our intense cold spells and humid July heat. In off-peak season, you’re less likely to wait weeks for an opening, and experienced installers have more time to handle system commissioning and code inspections correctly. Some contractors may even offer a small discount or be more flexible on scheduling outside peak demand. Prices for typical replacements ($5,300–$12,500 for central systems) may not change seasonally, but the peace of mind and installer attention absolutely do.
