Permits & regulations in Pennsylvania

In Pennsylvania, HVAC work isn’t “just a repair”—there are real credential and permit expectations that protect homeowners. HVAC contractors must hold a Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) registration through the Pennsylvania Attorney General’s Office. If gas appliances are involved (like furnace replacement), the installing technician must hold an active PA gas piping license. For air conditioning and heat pump refrigerant work, refrigerant handling requires EPA Section 608 certification—so you should ask for the technician’s EPA 608 card before any refrigerant is transferred.

Mechanical permits are required by most PA municipalities for HVAC replacement and new installation. A permit is typically not required for like-for-like filter changes or thermostat swaps, but replacement of equipment (air handler, furnace, condenser, heat pump) usually triggers permitting and inspection requirements. Because you’re in Lower Allen / Cumberland County, the township or borough you’re in can influence whether plan review is required when ductwork is added or electrical work is expanded—this can affect timeline and cost, even when equipment price is similar.

For rebates, check the PA Homeowner Rebate program and utility rebates from PPL Electric, PECO, and Columbia Gas for qualifying high-efficiency heat pumps and furnaces under the IRA Inflation Reduction Act programs. ENERGY STAR equipment may also qualify for federal tax credits up to 30% under Section 25C.

To verify a contractor, start at hic.attorneygeneral.gov for HIC registration, request the technician’s EPA 608 certificate, and review proof of insurance and workers’ compensation coverage. If you’re asked to pay in full before paperwork is issued, that’s a red flag—especially for permitting-heavy projects.

Should I repair or replace my HVAC system in Lower Allen?

If you’re seeing rusted drain pans, repeated capacitor/control failures, or you’re replacing parts every season, ask for a written assessment that includes equipment efficiency comparisons and a comfort performance check—not just a one-part replacement.

What is a SEER2 rating and why does it matter in Lower Allen?

SEER2 is the seasonal efficiency rating used for modern air conditioning and cooling performance. In Lower Allen and across Pennsylvania, SEER2 matters because you’re paying for cooling during humid summer stretches, and efficiency affects how often and how hard your system runs while maintaining comfort. Higher SEER2 generally means less electricity use for the same cooling demand, but the savings only show up if the equipment is properly sized and installed with correct airflow. If ductwork is leaky or returns are inadequate—common in older pre-1980 homes—real-world performance can fall short even with high-rated equipment.

When comparing options, SEER2 is one piece alongside setup and zoning. For central AC replacement on existing ducts, the typical SEER2 range in our market is about 16–20 SEER2, with installed pricing commonly discussed around $5,500–$12,000 depending on scope. In practice, we don’t sell SEER2 alone—we verify indoor coil/air handler match, measure airflow, and recommend duct sealing or balancing when needed.
