Permits & regulations in Pennsylvania

In Pennsylvania, HVAC work isn’t just “find a good tech”—it’s about hiring the right credentials and following local permit rules. First, Pennsylvania requires HVAC contractors to hold a Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) registration through the Pennsylvania Attorney General’s Office. For gas appliances, the installing technician must hold an active PA gas piping license. Refrigerant handling for AC and heat pump work also requires EPA Section 608 certification, so ask for the technician’s EPA 608 card before work begins.

Mechanical permits are required by most PA municipalities for HVAC replacement and new installations. A permit is typically not required for like-for-like filter changes or thermostat swaps, but permits are commonly triggered when you replace equipment, move electrical connections, change ductwork, or do refrigerant work. Always verify what applies with your specific Crafton township/borough process and the municipality issuing the inspection.

For incentives, check the PA Homeowner Rebate program and utility rebates (PPL Electric, PECO, Columbia Gas) for qualifying high-efficiency heat pumps and furnaces under IRA Inflation Reduction Act programs. ENERGY STAR-certified equipment may also qualify for federal tax credits up to 30% under Section 25C.

How to verify step-by-step: (1) confirm PA HIC registration at hic.attorneygeneral.gov; (2) request the technician’s EPA 608 certificate; (3) ask for certificate of insurance and proof of workers’ compensation—then verify the policy includes the job location and dates. If a contractor won’t provide these documents in writing, move on.

Heat pump vs. furnace + AC — what makes sense in Crafton?

Crafton’s four-season weather makes the “heat pump vs. furnace + AC” decision more nuanced than it is in milder parts of the country. In Allegheny County, winters include frequent sub-freezing stretches and humid summers bring comfort concerns that go beyond just temperature. Modern cold-climate air source heat pumps (CC-ASHP) can operate efficiently down to about -13°F, which makes them viable across most of the region. But in Crafton’s older housing stock—about 94.3% built before 1980—duct leakage, insulation gaps, and reduced airflow can undercut any system’s real performance if the installation isn’t built around your house.

When we evaluate options, we focus on distribution and controls: a ducted heat pump can deliver excellent year-round comfort when ducts are sealed and properly sized, but if your returns and supply paths are weak, it can feel “weak” even when the equipment is correctly rated. A furnace + central AC dual system is often a good fit when you have an existing gas line and want familiar winter output, but it does involve more moving parts and separate heating and cooling components.

Price-wise, the choice often comes down to whether you’ll pay to fix ducts and airflow so the system you install can actually earn its efficiency. For example, a ducted air source heat pump installation is commonly in the $7,500–$9,000 band, while a furnace + central AC dual system typically runs $7,500–$9,000 combined in many replace-and-upgrade scenarios. If your ducts need substantial correction, the “cheaper equipment” option can quickly lose value compared with the setup that has the best chance of performing after the ductwork work is done.

How to choose a HVAC installation contractor in Crafton

Picking an HVAC contractor in Crafton is one of the best ways to control both comfort and cost after installation. Start by verifying licensing and certification. For independent trades, we verify credentials using online registries: confirm the company’s Pennsylvania HIC registration through the Attorney General’s office, and ensure the installing technician can document required qualifications for the work (including EPA 608 for refrigerant-handling). Where gas is involved, confirm the technician holds the active PA gas piping license. If an installer can’t provide proof up front, that’s a red flag.

Next, get 2–3 written, itemized quotes. The quote should break out labor and materials—equipment model, installation scope (including duct sealing/balancing if needed), electrical changes, line sets, permits, and any disposal costs—rather than a single lump sum. Carefully read the scope: what’s excluded (drywall repairs, updated electrical service, duct replacement, diagnostics) and what’s included (load calculation, airflow balancing, startup testing)? Also ask whether the contractor provides documentation for insurance claims and what photos or test results you’ll receive after completion.

Warranty matters in Pennsylvania because older homes can “punish” poor installation: insist on a clear workmanship warranty and separate coverage for equipment and materials. For payments, never pay more than 10–15% upfront—then use a holdback until the job is complete and verified. Finally, demand a written arrival estimate and a completion window.

What is a SEER2 rating and why does it matter in Crafton?

SEER2 is an efficiency rating for air conditioners and certain cooling operations in modern equipment. However, SEER2 is only part of the story—system performance depends on correct sizing, proper refrigerant charge, sealed ductwork, and correct airflow across the coil and across the duct system. That’s especially important in Crafton because much of the housing stock is older, with ducts that may leak or be poorly balanced. When homeowners compare options, it’s common to evaluate central AC replacement equipment in efficiency tiers such as 16–20 SEER2 (often found in the $5,000–$13,000 installed range) versus higher-tier upgrades.
