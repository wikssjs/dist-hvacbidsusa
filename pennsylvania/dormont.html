Permits & regulations in Pennsylvania

In Pennsylvania, HVAC contracting and gas work aren’t “optional paperwork”—they’re part of keeping your system safe and compliant. First, Pennsylvania requires HVAC contractors to hold a Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) registration through the Pennsylvania Attorney General’s Office. If the job involves gas appliances (like a furnace swap), the installing technician must hold an active PA gas piping license. For AC and heat pump refrigerant work, the technician must also have EPA Section 608 certification—ask for the technician’s EPA 608 card before the work starts.

Mechanical permits are required by most PA municipalities for HVAC replacement and new installation. A permit is typically not required for like-for-like filter changes or thermostat swaps, but permits are commonly required for equipment replacement, ductwork modifications, and major electrical changes tied to new units. In Dormont, you should verify local permit rules with the borough (or ask your contractor which permits they are pulling).

Rebates and incentives are another reason to plan early. Check the PA Homeowner Rebate program and utility rebates (PPL Electric, PECO, Columbia Gas) for qualifying high-efficiency heat pumps and furnaces under IRA Inflation Reduction Act programs. ENERGY STAR-certified equipment may also qualify for federal tax credits up to 30% under Section 25C.

To verify credentials step-by-step: (1) confirm the contractor’s PA HIC registration at hic.attorneygeneral.gov; (2) request the technician’s EPA 608 certificate; (3) obtain a certificate of insurance showing general liability and proof of workers’ compensation coverage; and (4) keep copies of all permit submissions and inspection approvals with your job paperwork.

How to choose a HVAC installation contractor in Dormont

Choosing the right contractor in Dormont is less about ads and more about verifying credentials and accountability before anyone touches your home. Start by checking IICRC certification and PA licensing through online registries: confirm the contractor’s PA Home Improvement Contractor registration status, and verify the appropriate trade licensing credentials for the technician who will handle your installation. For refrigerant work, ask for the technician’s EPA 608 card. If your project involves gas, confirm the technician holds an active PA gas piping license.

Next, get 2–3 itemized written quotes—meaning line items for labor and materials, plus a clear description of what’s included in commissioning (thermostat setup, airflow tests, refrigerant charge verification, and cleanup). Avoid lump-sum quotes with vague scope. Read the scope section for exclusions: sometimes ducts, electrical upgrades, crane/hoisting, permit fees, or inaccessible crawlspace/attic labor are “added later.” Make sure insurance claim documentation requirements are addressed if you’re installing after damage.

Warranty details should be in writing: confirm the length of workmanship coverage (labor) and the equipment/material warranties. For payment scheduling, never pay more than 10–15% upfront; keep a holdback until the job is complete, inspected (when permits apply), and operating correctly. Finally, insist on a written timeline with arrival estimates and a completion window.

Do I need a permit to replace my HVAC in Dormont, PA?

In most cases, yes—HVAC equipment replacement typically requires a mechanical permit in Pennsylvania municipalities, including many situations in and around Dormont. Permits are commonly required when you replace the outdoor unit and indoor equipment, add new ductwork, modify electrical circuits, or make changes beyond a strictly like-for-like swap. The main exceptions are usually minor items like thermostat swaps or filter changes that don’t alter system components or electrical/gas connections.

Because permit rules can vary by borough and township, don’t assume—ask the contractor what they will pull and what inspections will be required. A reputable installer will include permit handling in the project plan and provide copies of submitted paperwork and final inspection results. If you’re budgeting for a $5,000–$13,000 central AC replacement or a $7,500–$9,000 heat pump installation, confirm whether permit fees and any electrical/duct repair permitting impacts are included in the quote.

What is a SEER2 rating and why does it matter in Dormont?

SEER2 (Seasonal Energy Efficiency Ratio 2) is the efficiency metric used for modern air conditioners and cooling portions of heat pumps. In practical terms for Dormont homeowners, it helps estimate how efficiently the system cools during Allegheny County’s humid summer months and how much electricity you’ll use at different outdoor temperatures. Because your home likely has older construction features—about 95% of Dormont homes were built before 1980 (U.S. Census Bureau, ACS 2024)—duct leakage and insulation gaps can reduce delivered comfort. Even with a high SEER2 unit, leaky ducts may cause the system to run longer than expected.

When comparing cooling equipment, look at SEER2 tier differences and also at how the contractor will verify airflow and refrigerant charge. For many installs, mainstream systems in the 16–20 SEER2 range can keep upfront costs moderate, while premium efficiency systems (often 18–20+ SEER2) can cost more but may reduce operating costs over time. If you’re replacing central AC and expect a $5,000–$13,000 project, we’ll discuss whether upgrading efficiency is worth it for your duct condition and your utility usage profile.
