What size AC system do I need for my Uniontown home?
Proper AC sizing is critical — oversized systems short-cycle and cause humidity problems; undersized systems can't keep up on hot PA days. The correct method is a Manual J load calculation performed by your licensed Uniontown HVAC contractor, which accounts for your home's square footage, insulation, window area, ceiling height, and local climate data. As a rough guide: 1 ton per 600-1,000 sq ft, but don't let a contractor quote based on square footage alone without a proper load calculation.
How long does AC installation take in Uniontown?
A standard central AC or heat pump installation in Uniontown — replacing an existing system — is typically completed in one day. New installations requiring ductwork additions may take 2-3 days. After installation, the system is pressure-tested, charged with refrigerant, and tested for proper operation before the crew leaves.

When should I replace vs. repair my AC in Uniontown, PA?

Permits & regulations in Pennsylvania

In Pennsylvania, HVAC work is governed by contractor licensing, technician credentialing, and local mechanical permit requirements. First, contractors performing home improvement work should have a Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) registration through the Pennsylvania Attorney General’s Office. For gas appliances (like furnace replacements), the installing technician must hold an active PA gas piping license. For air conditioning and heat pump refrigerant work, technicians must also be EPA Section 608 certified—ask to see the technician’s EPA 608 card before the work begins.

Most PA municipalities require mechanical permits for HVAC replacement and for new HVAC installations. A permit is typically not required for like-for-like filter changes or thermostat swaps, but replacing a furnace, changing an outdoor unit, modifying ductwork, or altering venting/combustion components generally triggers permitting. Because Uniontown and nearby boroughs/townships can vary in strictness, verify requirements with your local township or borough office.

For homeowners, also check eligibility for rebates and tax incentives. Look at the PA Homeowner Rebate program and utility incentives (including PPL Electric, PECO, and Columbia Gas where applicable) for qualifying high-efficiency heat pumps and furnaces under IRA Inflation Reduction Act programs. ENERGY STAR certified equipment may qualify for federal tax credits up to 30% under Section 25C.

Step-by-step contractor verification in Uniontown: (1) Confirm the contractor’s HIC registration at hic.attorneygeneral.gov. (2) Ask which technician will be on-site and request an EPA 608 card (for AC/heat pump refrigerant work). (3) Request certificate of insurance and workers’ compensation proof. (4) If gas piping applies, verify the technician’s PA gas piping license. (5) Ensure the permit process is handled by the contractor when required, with inspection dates coordinated with the schedule.

Heat pump vs. furnace + AC — what makes sense in Uniontown?

Uniontown’s weather makes the “heat pump vs. furnace + AC” decision more nuanced than in mild climates. You’ll see cold winter periods, plus hot, humid summers. Modern cold-climate air source heat pumps (CC-ASHP) can operate efficiently down to about -13°F, which makes them viable for most of Fayette County. The catch is that Uniontown’s older housing stock—about 92% of homes built before 1980—often has duct leakage and insulation gaps that reduce real delivered comfort and can lower effective system efficiency.

If your ducts are in good shape and your home is reasonably insulated, central AC combined with a furnace can still be a strong “dual system” approach. When we’re dealing with leaky duct runs, tight crawlspaces, or rooms that never feel right, a heat pump can deliver better comfort per dollar—especially when paired with an insulation and air sealing plan. For homes needing zone control or where ductwork would be expensive to retrofit, ductless mini-splits frequently make more sense than trying to push air through aging duct systems.

Concrete example: if you’re choosing between a dual-fuel style setup and a full furnace + AC path, the upfront gap can be meaningful. A furnace + central AC (dual system) commonly lands around $6,500–$8,000 combined for many typical Uniontown installs, while a ducted cold-climate heat pump install can be in the $6,500–$8,000 band as well—often making the decision hinge on duct performance, gas availability, and how you want to manage extreme-cold comfort. In homes where duct losses are severe, heat pump operating efficiency can be “spent” just moving air; in those cases, we may steer toward options that reduce duct dependence.

How to choose a HVAC installation contractor in Uniontown

Choosing the right contractor in Uniontown is mostly about proof and process—not brochures. Start by verifying licensing and certifications that apply to the exact work you’re authorizing. For IICRC-related certifications and workmanship credentials, ask what certification(s) are held and how they map to your system type; for PA licensing and compliance, confirm the contractor is properly registered for home improvement work. In Pennsylvania, you can check contractor credentials through official online registries; if you’re hiring for gas equipment, verify the installing technician’s active PA gas piping license.

Next, get 2–3 itemized written quotes—not lump sums. The quote should show a labor and materials breakdown, including equipment model numbers, refrigerant type, electrical components (disconnect/breaker as needed), filter/air handler or furnace details, condensate line plans, and any ductwork that is included or excluded. Carefully read the scope: is duct sealing included, are registers balanced, will they perform combustion testing for gas furnaces, and will they provide any required inspection documentation for permits?

Warranty and payment terms protect you. Confirm workmanship warranty length separately from equipment warranty, and verify who covers parts versus labor. For payment, avoid large deposits: never pay more than 10–15% upfront, and hold back funds until the job is complete and verified. Ask for an arrival window and a completion timeline in writing, especially since Fayette County projects commonly book out about 2–4 weeks during peak season.
