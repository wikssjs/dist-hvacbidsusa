What size AC system do I need for my Scranton home?
Proper AC sizing is critical — oversized systems short-cycle and cause humidity problems; undersized systems can't keep up on hot PA days. The correct method is a Manual J load calculation performed by your licensed Scranton HVAC contractor, which accounts for your home's square footage, insulation, window area, ceiling height, and local climate data. As a rough guide: 1 ton per 600-1,000 sq ft, but don't let a contractor quote based on square footage alone without a proper load calculation.

In Scranton's climate — hot, humid summers and cold winters — a well-maintained central AC system typically lasts 15-20 years. Gas furnaces last 20-25 years. Heat pumps last 15-20 years. Permits & regulations in Pennsylvania

In Pennsylvania, HVAC contractors and technicians must meet specific credential requirements, especially for gas and refrigerant work. Pennsylvania requires HVAC contractors to hold a Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) registration through the Pennsylvania Attorney General’s Office—verify it at hic.attorneygeneral.gov. If the job involves gas appliances, the installing technician must hold an active PA gas piping license. For AC and heat pump installations, technicians who handle refrigerant must have EPA Section 608 certification; ask to see the technician’s EPA 608 card.

Mechanical permits are typically required by most PA municipalities for HVAC replacement and new installation. A permit usually is not required for like-for-like filter changes or thermostat swaps, but it is required for changes that affect fuel lines, combustion components, refrigerant circuits, or major equipment replacement. Because Scranton and some nearby townships can add inspection steps (for example, condenser placement, electrical work, and gas line checks), confirm the permitting path with your local borough or township office before work begins.

For incentives, check the PA Homeowner Rebate program and utility rebates from providers such as PPL Electric, PECO, and Columbia Gas for eligible high-efficiency heat pumps and furnaces under IRA Inflation Reduction Act programs. ENERGY STAR equipment may also qualify for federal tax credits up to 30% under Section 25C.

To verify a contractor in Scranton, start with the PA HIC registry online, then ask for: (1) proof of HIC registration, (2) EPA 608 certification for the technician who will handle refrigerant, and (3) a current certificate of insurance plus workers’ compensation documentation. Don’t guess—request the insurance certificate and proof of WC coverage in writing, and match the named insured to the contractor doing the work.

Heat pump vs. furnace + AC — what makes sense in Scranton?

Scranton’s winters and summers make the decision between a heat pump and a furnace + central AC more nuanced than in milder regions. Pennsylvania commonly experiences cold snaps with sub-freezing periods, plus humid summers where cooling comfort matters. Modern cold-climate air source heat pumps (CC-ASHP) can operate efficiently down to about -13°F, making them viable across most of Lackawanna County. That said, Scranton’s older housing stock is the complication: 87.2% of homes were built before 1980 (U.S. Census Bureau, ACS 2024), so duct leakage, uneven insulation, and older air distribution can cut comfort and inflate run time long before a system ever reaches its efficiency rating on paper.

When ducts leak, a ducted heat pump may need longer run cycles to maintain indoor temperatures, which is why we prioritize sealing, balancing, and often returns placement before sizing final equipment. If you already have a dependable gas furnace setup, a dual system (heat pump plus gas backup) can protect comfort during the coldest hours while still delivering lower operating costs for most of the season. Conversely, if your home’s ducts are struggling and you’re not planning significant ductwork improvements, a furnace + AC may feel simpler—but the cost of maintaining two systems and the impact of leaky ducts on cooling still need to be addressed.

A concrete example: if your ductwork is in poor shape and duct repair pushes a central AC replacement from the typical $6,500–$15,000 band toward a larger airflow correction scope, the “gap” can be easier to justify for a ducted heat pump project in the $9,000–$20,000 planning range—especially when it also replaces furnace heat. In other words, the best financial decision in Scranton often comes down to how much duct and insulation work your home truly needs, not just which equipment type you prefer.

How to choose a HVAC installation contractor in Scranton

Choosing the right contractor in Scranton is the fastest way to avoid “cheap install, expensive problems” scenarios. Start with licensing and certifications. In Pennsylvania, confirm the contractor’s HIC registration via the Attorney General’s HIC lookup (hic.attorneygeneral.gov). For installation work that involves refrigerant handling, ask the technician for an EPA Section 608 card and confirm it’s associated with the person doing the refrigerant work. If gas work is part of your furnace or gas appliance scope, make sure the installing technician holds the appropriate PA gas piping license.

Next, get 2–3 itemized, written quotes—not lump sums. You want labor and materials broken out (equipment, lineset/ductwork components, labor hours, electrical/gas tie-ins, insulation, and testing). Read the scope carefully and confirm what’s excluded: duct sealing, duct resizing, permits, electrical upgrades, and refrigerant charge verification are common “surprise” items if they’re not clearly stated. For insurance and liability, ask whether any required documentation (and photos of key steps) will be included for your records.

Warranty matters in Scranton’s climate: ask for the workmanship warranty length (commonly separate from equipment coverage) and verify the equipment and materials warranties for the specific brand and model you’re buying. Payment schedule should be conservative—never pay more than 10–15% upfront, and hold back funds until the job is complete and tested. Finally, request a written arrival estimate and completion window, because peak-season lead times for full change-outs can stretch several weeks.

What is a SEER2 rating and why does it matter in Scranton?

SEER2 is the efficiency rating used for modern cooling equipment (like central air and many heat pump cooling modes). In Scranton, it matters because your system has to handle humid summer conditions, not just “cooling on paper.” A higher SEER2 can reduce energy use, but efficiency only delivers if the system is installed and matched correctly to your home’s airflow. That’s especially important in Lackawanna County’s older housing stock—87.2% of dwellings were built before 1980 (U.S. Census Bureau, ACS 2024)—where duct leakage and undersized or poorly arranged ductwork can cause cooling to underperform even with a high-efficiency unit.

When you’re comparing options, look beyond the SEER2 number and ask for commissioning/testing: duct airflow measurements, proper refrigerant charge, and airflow balance. That’s how you make the difference between an AC that merely “runs” and one that actually delivers comfort. As a reference point, a central AC replacement with working ducts is often estimated in the $6,500–$15,000 installed band; if duct repairs are required, the project can climb because you’re fixing the pathway that makes SEER2 count.
